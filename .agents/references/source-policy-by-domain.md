# Source Policy by Domain

Use this reference to propose a source boundary after the user states a goal. The lists are defaults to discuss, never universal authority rankings.

## General Rules

- Preserve user-specified sources as `user_primary` even when their evidence quality is limited.
- Treat authority, relevance, proximity, currency, version, jurisdiction, independence, and accessibility as separate dimensions.
- Explain weak, stale, conflicting, inaccessible, or superseded material; do not replace it silently.
- Prefer primary and official sources for facts about standards, laws, products, APIs, and current behavior.
- Add secondary synthesis when it materially improves interpretation, comparison, or pedagogy.
- A source policy defines allowed classes and constraints. A later supplemental shortlist identifies concrete additions.
- Record source role separately from authority. A `structural_baseline` source helps establish curriculum coverage; it does not automatically become factual evidence for publication claims.

## Knowledge Work

### Science, engineering, computing, and research

Prefer peer-reviewed journals, recognized conferences, authoritative textbooks, standards bodies, official specifications, official documentation, original datasets, and primary repositories pinned to versions or commits. Use preprints with clear status and caveats.

For a broad curriculum or textbook, use authoritative textbook tables of contents, recognized course syllabi, competency frameworks, and high-quality surveys or taxonomies to establish a structural coverage baseline. Triangulate across independent traditions or application areas when one source could define the field too narrowly.

### Mathematics and theoretical subjects

Prefer established textbooks, monographs, original papers where historically or technically important, lecture notes from recognized institutions, and sources that expose definitions and proofs at the required level.

### Medicine and health

Prefer current clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, major professional bodies, regulators, and high-quality peer-reviewed evidence. Record population, jurisdiction, date, evidence grade, and scope. Keep learning support distinct from personal medical diagnosis or treatment.

### Law, policy, and engineering standards

Prefer controlling statutes, regulations, cases, standards, official commentary, and exact jurisdiction and revision. Secondary analysis helps interpretation but does not silently replace the controlling text.

### Social science, business, and humanities

Balance primary sources, recognized scholarly work, reputable datasets, institutional reports, and competing interpretations. Record methodology, population, time period, incentives, and viewpoint where they affect meaning.

### Courses, videos, and informal learning

Treat the specified course as the instructional spine when the user chooses it. Supplement important claims with authoritative original or reference sources when allowed. Preserve timestamps and episode or lesson identity.

## Product Work

Use the sources closest to the requested product and operating environment:

1. user requirements and existing product records;
2. official product documentation, source code, schemas, changelogs, standards, and contracts;
3. direct measurements, logs, user research, and representative data;
4. reputable analyses, comparisons, or community evidence;
5. general background sources.

Version and compatibility claims require sources matching the actual environment. Recommendations should disclose uncertainty, incentives, and materially different alternatives.

## Constraints to Confirm

Confirm only constraints that matter to the result: language, date range, version, geography, jurisdiction, budget, paywall or access, confidentiality, licensing, required viewpoints, and explicit exclusions. If the user names no source, propose a reasonable policy and ask in the compact intake rather than assuming a field-specific corpus.
